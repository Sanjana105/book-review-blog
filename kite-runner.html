The Kite Runner

Book Review | Khaled Hosseini

📖 About the Book

The Kite Runner is a novel written by Khaled Hosseini. The story focuses on the friendship between Amir, the son of a wealthy man, and Hassan, the son of his father's servant.

The story begins in Afghanistan in the 1970s and follows the characters through major changes in their lives and their country.

📚 Story

Amir and Hassan grow up together in Kabul. They share a strong friendship, and kite fighting becomes an important part of their childhood.

However, an event following a kite-fighting tournament changes their relationship and leaves Amir with a deep sense of guilt.

Later, political and social changes force Amir and his father to leave Afghanistan and build a new life in America. As Amir grows older, he eventually gets an opportunity to confront his past and seek redemption.

💡 Major Themes

  • Friendship
  • Betrayal
  • Guilt
  • Family
  • Sacrifice
  • Forgiveness
  • Redemption
  • The effects of war and social divisions

⭐ My Review

The Kite Runner was one of the most emotionally powerful books among the five. The relationship between Amir and Hassan makes the story memorable, while Amir's guilt gives the novel a strong emotional centre.

I also liked how the story connects personal experiences with the changing history of Afghanistan. It shows how decisions made in childhood can continue to affect people many years later.

📝 Conclusion

The Kite Runner is a powerful story about friendship, mistakes and the possibility of redemption. It is emotionally challenging but also thought-provoking, making it a memorable reading experience.

My Rating

★★★★★
